Help me interpret the trace

First compare demand with speed before the load arrives. Then inspect the error after 6 s. A persistent error under load calls for integral action. For the force prediction, balance the constant load against the motor force and viscous drag.

Predict, run, inspect

  1. Before the load step, predict whether the controller can hold 0.75 m/s. Inspect drive force and tracking error at 5 s.
  2. At 6 s a resisting force is applied. Compare proportional gains of 8, 24 and 48. Which reduces tracking error without spending longer at the force limit?
  3. Start a separate coast-down run with initial speed 1 m/s, load 0 N, mass 8 kg and drag 4 N·s/m. The speed should fall to about 0.368 m/s after one time constant (2 s).
Equations, sampling and units

The drive and load act on one equivalent mass: m dv/dt = Fdrive − Fload − bv. The force is held constant during each sample, with exact integration of viscous drag and position. Belt travel stops at zero speed. At each control tick the proportional-integral (PI) controller evaluates force from speed error and the stored integral, clamps it to ± the force limit, then integrates error unless saturation would increase. A demand or load change begins at the first sample on or after its specified time. The final partial interval ends at the exact run duration.

Integrated absolute error is the sum of |speed error| × sample duration, in metres. These parameters describe a synthetic lumped drive. No hardware connection or external computing engine is used.
